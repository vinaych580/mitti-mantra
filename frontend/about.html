About Mitti Mantra

A farm planner that shows its working

The problem

Small and medium farmers in India decide what to sow with scattered information about soil, weather, input costs and mandi prices. The result is avoidable yield loss, unstable profit and more financial risk.

Who it is designed for

Ramesh is a composite persona, not a real user: 38, three semi-irrigated acres in Telangana, choosing between cotton, soybean and maize, more at home in Telugu and Hindi than in English.

He wants the best profit per acre, to avoid a failed crop in a bad-rain year, and a risk figure he can understand.

Farm advisors, who serve many farmers and compare scenarios side by side, matter just as much.

The approach

Simulation first, machine learning on top

Every estimate has a deterministic path that can be explained: crop parameters, soil, weather and costs worked through a simulator. Trained models are used where they beat simple baselines; where they do not, the baseline is served.

Every answer says where it came from

Every response carries a source field (prediction_source) that says whether a number came from a trained model, the district's own record or the rule-based fallback. The screens show it next to the number.

Honest labelling

Sample data is labelled as sample. Old prices say how old they are. When the yield model missed its accuracy target on unseen years, the app kept serving each district's five-year average instead.

There are no users and no pilot yet. The design choices are informed guesses until farmers have tried the planner on a phone in Telugu or Hindi.

How it is built

One repository and no build step. Vercel serves the pages as static files and runs the API as one Python function.

The models

Yield: the model beat each district's five-year average on every test split, but not by the 10% asked of it on unseen years. So the app serves the five-year average, with a calibrated 80% range.

Price: a separate model for each forecast horizon, checked against simple baselines crop by crop. Where it does not beat them, the forecast says what it uses instead.

Defects found and fixed

In November 2026 every API route was probed with edge cases and the app was checked at phone width. The register lists 34 defects. These are the ones that most changed what a farmer sees.

  • N-1, N-2

    The rainfall and irrigation controls did nothing: no rain scored slightly higher than normal rain.

    Scenarios now respond to water through the FAO-33 yield response, labelled as a rule-based response.

  • N-3, N-4

    Crops that lose money were shown as top recommendations.

    Losing crops are never shown beside profitable ones. When every crop loses, the app says so and why.

  • N-5

    Yield and weather history for 22 of 30 regions were random numbers.

    Replaced with public records: district yields (DES), mandi prices (Agmarknet) and weather (NASA POWER). A validation gate rejects synthetic rows.

  • N-7

    The default budget was below the cost of growing most major crops.

    The default is now the typical cost for the district and season.

  • N-9

    Price forecasts ran from data a year old and were marked high confidence.

    Forecasts say how old their data is, and confidence is capped at low when it is stale.

  • N-14

    No security headers on any response.

    Security headers on every response; a page content policy runs in report-only mode.

  • N-16, N-34

    No CI, and the test suite was failing.

    The suite passes and runs on every push and pull request.

  • #14

    The dashboard showed a total loss as its top pick.

    It now shows the answer that every option loses money.

  • #20

    Sample tractor listings carried realistic mobile numbers, and the booking screen said to call them.

    Sample listings show no phone number, and a sample booking says that nothing was booked.

Still open: Kannada and Tamil lack some newer strings, and crop costs are on a 2021 basis while prices are from 2025–26, which flatters every return a little. The full register and the notes for each phase are in the repository.
